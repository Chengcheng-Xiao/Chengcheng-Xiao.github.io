In 2010, Joydeep Bhattacharjee et al published a paper titled "Wannier orbital
overlap population (WOOP), Wannier orbital position population (WOPP) and the
origin of anomalous dynamical charges" [Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys., 2010, 12,
1564–1570](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2010/cp/b918890h). In
which, they proposed a method to decompose certain real-space observables (e.g.,
charge, dipole moment) into contributions from atomic-like Wannier orbitals
(AOs) and molecular-like Wannier orbitals (MOs). 

This method serves as an direct extension of the Crystal Orbital Hamilton
Populations (COHP) and Crystal Orbital Overlap Populations (COOP) methods (see,
for example, [J. Phys. Chem., 1993, 97 (33),
    8617–8624](https://doi.org/10.1021/j100135a014).), which are done in energy
    space. 

In this post, I will briefly introduce the theory behind WOOP and WOPP and show
how to use them with Wannier basis (for both AOs and MOs) to decompose the Born
effective charge (BEC) of a ferroelectric material PbTiO<sub>3</sub>.

Wannier function $\phi_n$ sitting in cell $\mathbf{R}$ is defined as

$$
\phi_{\mathbf{R},n}(\mathbf{r}) =\frac{V}{(2\pi)^3} \int_\text{BZ} e^{-i \mathbf{k}. \mathbf{R}} \sum_m U^{\mathbf{k}}_{mn} \psi_{\mathbf{k}, m} (\mathbf{r}) d\mathbf{k},
$$ 

where $\psi_{\mathbf{k}, m}$ is Bloch states at $\mathbf{k}$ point and band
number $m$, $U$ is a unitary matrix that mixes the Bloch states. The constraint
here is that $U$ needs to be square, meaning the number of $m$ needs to be the
same as $n$. orbital-like Wannier functions by setting $m$ to also include the
anti-bonding like states in the unoccupied manifold.

The main goal of WOOP and WOPP is to decompose Wannier charge and dipole which
are usually described by molecular-like Wannier orbitals (MOs) using a set of
atomic-like Wannier orbitals (AOs).

$$
\sum_{j=1}^N S_{mj} C_{jI} = \braket{\phi_m|W_I}
$$

Here, unlike what Joydeep et al did, the AOs and MOs are all Wanneir functions
from the same set of Bloch functions (MOs are restricted to be occupied Bloch
states only and AO uses some empty bands). This means that the AOs forms a
orthonormal basis, i.e., $S$ is identity. Because of this, the projection
coefficients $C$ are simply
$$

Again, because the AOs and MOs are all Wanneir functions from the same global
set of Bloch functions, the underlying basis (Bloch states) is the same, and the
$U$ matrix for the MOs can be zero-padded to be the same shape as $U$ matrix for
the AOs. Hence, 

$$
 C_{m,I} =\braket{\phi_m|W_I} =\int_\mathbf{k} \sum_n U^{\mathrm{AO}*}_{nm} U^{\mathrm{MO}}_{nI} e^{i\mathbf{k}.(\mathbf{R}_m-\mathbf{R}_I)} d\mathbf{k}.
$$

Note that here $I$ and $m$ are composite indices that also specifies which cell
does these Wannier functions sits $R_m$ and $R_l$.

## Wannier Orbital Overlap Population

With $C$ calculated, the charge of a Wannier functions carries can be expressed as
$$
\rho_I = \braket{W_I | W_I},
$$

With $C$ calculated, $\rho^I$ can be decomposed into the Wannier Orbital Overlap
Population (WOOP) $B_{lm}^I$

$$
B_{lm}^I = C_{Il} S_{lm} C_{Im}  = \delta_{l,m} C_{Il} C_{Im}.
$$

Here, because AOs form an orthonormal basis, $B^I$ is a diagonal matrix.
Remember MOs $W$ describes only the occupied bands, the total charge of the
system can be represented as

$$
\rho = \sum_I \braket{W_I | W_I} = \sum_{I,l,m} B_{lm}^I   = \sum_{I} \mathrm{Tr}(B^I).  
$$

## Wannier Orbital Position Population 

Similar to Wannier charge, the Wannier charge centre (WCC)

$$
\braket{\mathbf{r}}_I = \braket{W^I |\mathbf{r}| W^I}
$$

can be decomposed into the Wannier Orbital Position Population (WOPP) $D_{lm}^I$,

$$
D_{lm}^I = C^*_{Il} \phi^*_l \mathbf{r} \phi_m C_{Im} = C^*_{Il} \left[ \phi^*_l \mathbf{r} \phi_m\right] C_{Im} = C_{Il} \mathbf{r}_{lm} C_{Im}
$$

and $\sum_{l,m} D_{lm}^I$ should give $\braket{\mathbf{r}}_I$.

The Wannier90 provides finite difference method to calculate the position matrix
elemeents $\mathbf{r}_{lm}$. However, by defult for finite systems like 2D
slabs, the translation invariance is broken for the off-diagonal elements. To
solve this, Jae-Mo Lihm et al proposed an updated method to calculate the 
position matrix elements: see [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22614).
 

The developers have also implemented this method in the latest version of Wannier90
(v4.0.0), and the relevant keywords are [`transl_inv_full`](https://wannier90.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/postw90/postw90params/?h=transl_inv_full#logical-transl_inv_full) for postw90, and
and [`higher_order_n`](https://wannier90.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/wannier90/parameters/?h=high#integer-higher_order_n) for wannier90.

## BEC decomposition 

The electric contribution to the dipole moment can be The electric contribution
to the dipole moment can be obtained from WCC

$$
\mathbf{d}^\mathrm{elect.}  = -2\sum_{I\in\text{occ.}} 
 \braket{\mathbf{r}}_I  =-2\sum_{I\in\text{occ.}}  \sum_{l,m} D_{lm}^I,
$$

where the factor $-2$ comes from the fact that electron has negative charge and
each Wannier function can be occupied by two electrons. Note that here
$\mathbf{d}$ has the unit of `abs(electron)Å`. The total dipole moment is:

$$
\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{d}^\mathrm{elect.} + \mathbf{d}^\mathrm{ion.}
$$

where $\mathbf{d}^\mathrm{ion.}$ is the ionic contribution.

<!-- With WOPP ($D_{lm}^I$) calculated, $\mathbf{d}^\mathrm{elect.}$ can be easily obtained.  -->
For periodic system, the centro-symmetric contribution needs to be subtracted from teh 

$$
\mathbf{d}= \mathbf{d}_\mathrm{FE} - \mathbf{d}_\mathrm{Centro.}  
$$

We are now ready to derive the Born effective charge (BEC) tensor $Z$

$$
Z_{\kappa,ij} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{d}_i}{\partial \mathbf{u}_{\kappa, j}}
$$

where $\mathbf{u}_{\kappa, j}$ denotes the distortion of atom $\kappa$ along
direction $j$, and $\mathbf{d}_i$ is the induced change in the total dipole
moment. 

Assuming the ferroelectric distortion is within the linear regime, the derivative
can be discretised as

$$
Z_{\kappa,ij} = \frac{\mathbf{d}^\mathrm{elect.}_\mathrm{FE} - \mathbf{d}^\mathrm{elect.}_\mathrm{Centro.} }{\mathbf{u}_{\kappa, j}} + Z^\mathrm{ion}
$$

We can express $\mathbf{d}$ with WOPP $D$

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\kappa,ij} &= \sum_{I\in\text{occ.}}  \sum_{l,m} Z^\mathrm{elect.}_{\kappa,ij} + Z^\mathrm{ion} \\
&=\frac{\left[ -2\sum_{I\in\text{occ.}}  \sum_{l,m} D_{lm}^{I,\mathrm{FE}} - -2\sum_{I\in\text{occ.}}  \sum_{l,m} D_{lm}^{I,\mathrm{CENT}} \right]_i}{\mathbf{u}_{\kappa, j}}  + Z^\mathrm{ion},
\end{aligned}
$$

and $Z^\mathrm{elect.}_{\kappa,ij}$ can be further decomposed as

$$
Z^\mathrm{elect.,I}_{\kappa,ij,lm}= -2 (D_{lm}^{I,\mathrm{FE}}-D_{lm}^{I,\mathrm{CENT}})_i/{\mathbf{u}_{\kappa, j}}
$$

where depending on which atom $l$ and $m$ belongs to, we can identify specific
contributions to the BEC and gain more insights into the origin of
ferroelectrics.
